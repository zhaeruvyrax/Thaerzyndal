# Thaerzyndal

> The orchard bore one glass fruit. Inside it, winter waited for a tree.

## The Garden beyond the Sundial

Thaerzyndal begins where a sundial's shadow ends. The garden can be entered only at an hour the dial does not mark. Visitors who arrive too early find a plain stone wall. Visitors who arrive too late find their own footprints returning from the other side.

The orchard contains trees with silver bark and branches that curve toward the ground. Most bear no fruit. Their shadows, however, are heavy with round shapes that move when the branches remain still.

At the center grows a tree with a single glass fruit. Inside it, snow falls across a small empty field. The field contains a sundial, and the sundial's shadow points toward a wall that appears nowhere else in the garden.

## The Gardener's Three Gloves

The gardener keeps three gloves hanging beside an iron gate. Two are sized for human hands. The third is long and narrow, with six fingers ending in small polished claws.

Nobody has seen the gardener wear that glove. Nobody has seen the gardener's bare hands. The orchard is tended before sunrise, when visitors hear the scraping of metal and the slow rustling of wings somewhere between the trees.

A guest once remained awake to watch. He saw a figure kneeling beside the central tree, holding its shadow against the soil. The figure's head was bowed beneath a hood, but its reflection in the glass fruit had horns.

The guest looked away. When he looked again, the snow inside the fruit had stopped falling. A small footprint stood beside the reflected sundial, shaped exactly like the third glove.

## The Harvest of Unwritten Names

Each autumn, visitors leave a name upon a piece of paper beneath the gate. The gardener collects the papers without reading them and places them in shallow trenches between the roots.

The following spring, one shadow-fruit appears for every name. They cannot be picked. They vanish if touched with a living hand. A visitor may stand beneath one and hear a voice describing a place where that name has never been spoken.

A woman left her own name twice. Two fruits appeared upon the same shadow, each speaking with her voice. One described her childhood home. The other described a garden whose gate held two gloves and a human hand.

She withdrew both papers. The fruits remained, but their voices began speaking to each other in a language she almost remembered.

## A Record of the Iron Gate

The gate has no hinges. It stands open without moving and closed without meeting the wall. Its state depends upon which glove a visitor remembers first.

The gardeners of neighboring estates attempted to draw its mechanism. Their drawings showed roots, wings, and the outline of a small field covered in snow. No drawing showed iron.

One drawing included a fourth glove. It was hanging inside the glass fruit, above the sundial. The artist insisted that it had appeared while he slept, though the ink was still wet upon his fingers.

He returned to the orchard carrying the drawing. The gate's shadow reached across the paper and closed around the fourth glove. When he unfolded the sheet afterward, he found only a narrow strip of winter.

## The Inscription within the Snow

**Thaer — zyn — dal — orvae.**

These sounds are visible as letters when frost gathers upon the glass fruit. They are heard as a quiet knocking when the glass is warm. The gardener never answers the knocking, but sometimes places the third glove beneath the tree.

A child asked whether the glove belonged to the voice inside. The gardener replied that voices did not keep their hands in the same season as their bodies.

The child waited beside the fruit until dusk. Through the glass, she saw a pale dragon circling the snowy field. Its wings moved without disturbing the falling snow. One claw held a sundial, which cast the shadow of a child waiting beneath a tree.

She returned to the gate without asking another question.

## The Conversation beneath the Branches

> When will the fruit become ripe?
>
> When the field remembers its first root.
>
> Which name belongs beneath the gate?
>
> The one whose voice has not reached the shadow.
>
> Why are there three gloves for two hands?
>
> Because someone has already left the garden wearing winter.

The woman with the two names recorded these answers. They came from the shadow-fruits, spoken in alternating voices. She left the record in the trenches, folded around a small shard of clear glass.

The next spring, no new fruit appeared there. A narrow gate emerged in the shadow instead, carrying an empty hook where a glove might once have hung.

## The Hour outside the Dial

The orchard still grows beyond the sundial. Its gardener tends the roots before dawn and leaves the third glove hanging where no visitor can overlook it.

The glass fruit remains upon the central tree. Sometimes its snowy field shows a small green shoot. Sometimes the shoot casts the shadow of a wing. The snow continues falling around it without touching its leaves.

Thaerzyndal waits at an hour that has no mark upon the stone. Those who leave the orchard find the wall behind them unchanged, except for a small warm place shaped like a hand with six fingers.

*The glass fruit holds the season that has not found its body.*
